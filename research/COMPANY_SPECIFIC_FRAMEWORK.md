# Samsung Electronics Company-Specific Valuation Framework

## Core analytical question

Samsung is not one earnings stream. Its valuation depends on the mix and durability of memory profits, the cost of restoring foundry economics, the cycle-normalized cash earnings of DX and SDC, and the quality of Harman's automotive growth. The model should value each distinct engine with a metric suited to its economics, then bridge to common equity without counting the same cycle upside twice.

## Segment decision tree

| Segment / sub-engine | Operating questions | Preferred valuation lens | Evidence that changes the view |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBM | Customer qualification by generation, stack/thermal yield, advanced packaging capacity, qualified share, contract mix, gross margin and customer concentration | Normalized segment EV/EBIT or scenario range; SOTP contribution | Confirmed qualification and volume, yield ramp, customer breadth, realized margin, supply additions |
| Server DRAM | Bit shipments, ASP, DDR5/high-capacity mix, cloud capex, contract terms, inventory and competing supply | Cycle-normalized EV/EBIT; separate from HBM | Server share of bit shipments, ASP/mix, inventory and disciplined capacity |
| Client/mobile DRAM | Smartphone/PC demand, LPDDR mix, customer inventory, spot-contract spread and bit supply | Lower-cycle EV/EBIT range; avoid peak EPS PER | End-market sell-through, OEM inventories and sustained price/margin through a cycle |
| NAND / eSSD | Enterprise SSD mix, QLC adoption, NAND supply discipline, utilization, ASP and write-down risk | Mid-cycle EV/EBIT; floor/capacity scenario if earnings are depressed | eSSD share, contract pricing, inventory, utilization and sustained cash returns |
| Foundry / S.LSI | Node-specific yield and utilization, customer concentration, external sales, advanced packaging, wafer starts and capex burden | Standalone scenario SOTP; do not force PER during losses | Verified yields, external design wins moving to volume, utilization, loss reduction and cash payback |
| DX: MX / networks | Smartphone units and ASP, premium mix, component costs, launch cycle, channel inventory and service attach | Normalized EBIT / EPS; brand OEMs are demand references, not direct peers | Sell-through, product mix, margin through the cycle and inventory normalization |
| DX: VD / DA | TV/appliance volumes, product mix, panel/input cost, promotion and channel inventory | Conservative normalized EBIT / cash earnings | Mix and promotion, inventory, margins outside launch or replacement peaks |
| SDC | Mobile OLED volume, customer concentration, panel utilization, IT/auto OLED ramp, capex and depreciation | Normalized segment EBIT / asset return | Customer diversification, yield/utilization, non-mobile OLED shipments and returns on new lines |
| Harman | Automotive backlog, design wins converting to revenue, content per vehicle, software mix, margins and working capital | Normalized EBIT or cash earnings with separate growth case | Backlog conversion, recurring/software mix, cash conversion and acquisition integration |

## Memory valuation build: separate economics, not one uplift

The existing 2027E memory build assigns 25T KRW EBIT across HBM/server/client-mobile DRAM/NAND/other and applies 9.0x/7.0x/4.5x/3.0x/2.5x. These are **scenario multiples**, not observed product-level market quotations or statistically established fair-value factors. The weighted result is about 6.2x EV/EBIT. The product buckets must sum to 100% and the weighted multiple must reconcile to that sum.

Replace fixed narrative uplifts with a transparent factor card for each product: (1) normalized margin and cash conversion, (2) cycle amplitude and earnings duration, (3) customer qualification/switching costs, (4) supply response/capacity discipline, (5) concentration and contract visibility, (6) incremental ROIC versus reinvestment, and (7) customer/platform value capture. A factor changes the scenario multiple only when supported by dated evidence. For each factor show evidence, direction, confidence, counter-evidence and next observable trigger. Do not award a premium for “AI exposure” alone.

Avoid double counting: if higher HBM shipments/margins raise forecast EBIT, the multiple should reflect only risk/duration/value-capture differences beyond that forecast. A higher share of HBM EBIT also changes the weighted multiple automatically; do not apply a second consolidated HBM premium.

## Foundry and loss-making operations

Do not assign a positive PER to a loss-making foundry forecast. Maintain three explicit states: (a) continued loss/high capital intensity, (b) break-even with stable yield and customer loading, (c) positive normalized return on invested capital. Use scenario enterprise value and explicit cash burn/capex burden; an unverified customer announcement is not equivalent to volume revenue. TSMC is an operating/technology benchmark, not a plug-in multiple for Samsung Foundry because scale, utilization, customer mix, margin and node mix differ.

## Enterprise-to-equity bridge and corporate items

For every segment value, use a consistent EV/EBIT or EV/EBITDA basis and subtract corporate costs, net debt, leases if treated consistently, and non-controlling interests; add surplus cash and non-operating investments only once. Avoid counting cash inside segment multiples. Keep treasury shares / diluted shares and cross-holdings aligned with the valuation date.

## Cycle normalization and falsification

Peak memory earnings are not terminal earnings. A base case requires a through-cycle view of ASP, bit supply, depreciation, maintenance/growth CAPEX, inventory and R&D. Downside signals include inventory rising faster than bit shipments, capacity additions outpacing demand, falling utilization or price, delayed yields, rising customer concentration, foundry loss persistence, or strong reported EBIT with weak free cash flow. Upside requires realized mix, margins and cash conversion, not product roadmaps alone.
